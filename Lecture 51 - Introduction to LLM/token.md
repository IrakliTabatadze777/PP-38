At its most basic level, a **token** is something that serves as a **representation of something else**—like a unit of value, a piece of data, or a right to access a service. 

Because the word is used in several different fields today, its exact meaning depends on the context. Here are the four most common ways "token" is used:

---

### 1. In Artificial Intelligence & Natural Language Processing (AI/LLM)
If you are asking in the context of ChatGPT or AI, a token is **a chunk of text that an AI reads and processes**. 

AI models don't read whole words the way humans do; they break text down into smaller pieces called tokens.
* **A token can be:** A whole word, part of a word, or even a single character/punctuation mark.
* **Example:** The sentence *"Learning is fun!"* might be broken into 4 tokens: `["Learn", "ing", " is", " fun!"]`
* **Rule of thumb:** In English, **1 token is roughly equal to 0.75 words** (or about 4 characters). 1,000 tokens is roughly 750 words.
* **Why it matters:** AI companies (like OpenAI) charge based on the number of tokens processed, and AI models have a maximum limit of tokens they can remember at one time (known as the *context window*).

---

### 2. In Cryptocurrency & Blockchain
In the world of Web3 and crypto, a token is a **digital asset** that exists on an existing blockchain. 

* **Coin vs. Token:** 
  * A **Coin** has its own independent blockchain (e.g., Bitcoin, Ethereum).
  * A **Token** is built *on top* of an existing blockchain (e.g., tokens built on the Ethereum network like Uniswap, Tether, or Chainlink).
* **Types of Crypto Tokens:**
  * **Fungible Tokens:** Inter-changeable tokens used like money (e.g., Stablecoins like USDC).
  * **Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs):** Unique digital tokens representing ownership of a specific item, like digital art, real estate, or a domain name.
  * **Utility Tokens:** Give users access to a specific product or service within a blockchain ecosystem.

---

### 3. In Cybersecurity & Computing
In computer security, a token is a **digital "pass" or credential** used to prove who you are.

* **Authentication Tokens:** When you log into a website (like logging in with your Google account), the server gives your browser a hidden "token." This token proves you are logged in so you don't have to type your password on every new page you click.
* **API Tokens:** A secret string of code given to developers so their software can safely talk to another service.
* **Hardware Tokens:** A physical device (like a key fob or YubiKey) that generates a 6-digit code for Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).

---

### 4. In Everyday Life (Real World)
Historically, a token is a physical object used instead of real money.
* **Examples:** Arcade tokens, subway tokens, casino chips, or board game pieces (like the top hat in Monopoly). They hold no intrinsic value outside of the specific system where they are used.
